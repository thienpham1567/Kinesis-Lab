## General principles and evidence boundaries

The [2018 Delphi consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29025044/) describes expert agreement on a cluster of findings. It explicitly does not expect a fixed point location or referral area. It is not a sensitivity/specificity study. The [palpation review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18503816/) and [2021 low-back palpation review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34446040/) support uncertainty about reproducibility and validity, rather than the former “golden sign” language.

The [historical review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4508225/) introduction, role-of-MTrPs section and conclusion were read. They describe continuing uncertainty about mechanism and the relation between points and pain. The [nine-person microdialysis study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18164325/) describes biochemical group differences; it cannot verify a local energy crisis, a palpable physical lesion, or all muscles' mechanisms in an individual.

The [Cheatham abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26618062/) reports short-term ROM/recovery findings and no agreed optimal program. The [Behm & Wilke abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31256353/) discusses possible mechanisms and insufficient evidence for release of restrictions as the principal explanation. Neither certifies this app's specific pain maps, a common safe pain score, sustained ischemic compression, a mandatory activation sequence, recurrence within 24 hours, or a five-minute activation window. Those prescriptions and certainty claims were removed.

The app's conservative practice boundary is optional, comfortable surface self-care when appropriate; avoid deep neck, axillary, abdominal/groin, deep flank or nerve-provoking pressure. This is an editorial safety limit pending expert review, not an invented evidence-based dosing protocol. [NCCIH guidance](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/massage-therapy-what-you-need-to-know) describes rare significant harms, including nerve injuries, and advises against delaying assessment. The [brachial plexus case report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25212519/) shows a possible harm, not its population frequency. [Psoas anatomy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK535418/) supports its deep relations to organs, vessels and nerves; a fixed distance from the navel is not a safe target.

Safety text distinguishes urgent assessment of suspected [DVT](https://111.wales.nhs.uk/encyclopaedia/d/article/dvt%28deepveinthrombosis%29) from emergency chest/breathing symptoms. It includes new urinary retention and saddle sensory changes from [North Bristol's cauda equina guidance](https://www.nbt.nhs.uk/our-services/a-z-services/neurosurgery/neurosurgery-patient-information/same-day-emergency-clinic-sdec-cauda-equina-syndrome-sec). [Chest-pain guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/chest-pain/) informs emergency symptoms. There is no requirement to wait for every red flag or complete an SMR trial before referral.

## Source access and review status

`src/lib/clinical/evidence-trigger.ts` contains 23 source entries with exact titles, URLs, the 2026-10-19 check date, actual access scope, claim mappings and limits. Most peer-reviewed sources were read at abstract level; publisher or PMC full texts were not silently assumed to be read. PMC requests for Cheatham, Bron and Nolet returned a browser challenge, so their records explicitly say full text was unavailable at this check. Narrative-review sections and the cited public-guidance sections were accessible.

The historical Travell/Janda exports remain only for API compatibility and are explicitly labeled unaccessed for detailed verification. They are not used in the active records' evidence lists. No protected atlas figures, full text or patient material was copied.

General and every muscle-level evidence record remains limited. Source comparison, software tests and plausibility do not establish clinical validation. The exact diagrams, example movements, individual self-care choices and all content still require independent expert review before any clinical-validation claim.
