# Motor control / movement control (kiểm soát chuyển động) in PT, rehab and exercise science

Research date: 2026-10-16. Some primary full texts could not be fetched; PMC returned a reCAPTCHA. Where only an abstract or a secondary summary was seen, the finding says so. Items recalled from domain knowledge but not verified this session are in the Gaps sections, never under Cited Findings.

## Q1. Theory: motor control vs motor learning, key models, principles of practice

### Takeaway
Modern PT motor control is built on Shumway-Cook & Woollacott's systems/task-oriented model. In that model, movement comes from the interaction of the individual, the task and the environment. On the learning side, the strongest evidence for app features (feedback and cueing logic) supports three things: external-focus cues (meta-analytic g of about 0.26 for performance and about 0.58 for retention/transfer), random/variable practice for retention (a moderate effect that is weaker in applied settings and in children), and OPTIMAL-theory factors (autonomy, enhanced expectancies). Schmidt's schema theory is historically central but has been challenged.

### Cited Findings
- **Systems / task-oriented approach (Shumway-Cook & Woollacott, *Motor Control: Translating Research into Clinical Practice*).** The textbook connects systems theory of motor control to clinical examination and treatment. It treats observed movement as shaped by factors in the individual (sensory, motor and cognitive impairments) and also by the task and the environment. The clinical approach is called "task-oriented". — [ProQuest review record](https://proquest.com/scholarly-journals/motor-control-theory-practical-applications-ed-2/docview/223115309/se-2). Caveat: the other search hits were study aids or spam PDFs. Verify the exact definitions against the 5th edition (2016) or a later edition.
- **Schmidt's schema theory (1975), "A schema theory of discrete motor skill learning".** It was proposed because Adams' closed-loop theory had logical problems, storage problems (one memory trace per movement) and a "novelty" problem. It posits a recall memory (or schema) that produces movement and a recognition memory that evaluates whether the response was correct. It predicts that varied practice speeds up learning of the rule linking movement parameters to outcomes. — [Schmidt summary, U. Houston course PDF](https://grants.hhp.uh.edu/clayne/4315videos/KIN4315vid/6schmidt.pdf); [OUHSC summary](https://ouhsc.edu/bserdac/dthompso/web/mtrlrng/schmidt.htm)
- **Critiques of schema theory.** Sherwood & Lee (2003, RQES) note that findings on mental practice, observational learning, augmented feedback and variability of practice are inconsistent with schema theory. — [Sherwood & Lee 2003 record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4008365); ["Motor schema theory after 27 years" (Schmidt 2003)](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/3036823?lng=en)
- **Contextual interference (blocked vs random practice), Brady 2004 meta-analysis.**
  - Data: 61 studies and 139 effect sizes, with an overall ES of about .38.
  - Settings: the effect is larger in basic/lab research (.57) than in applied research (.19).
  - Age: it is larger in adults (.50) than in children (.10).
  - Power: mean study power was only .43.
  - Pattern: random practice impairs acquisition but improves retention and transfer.
  — [Brady 2004 PDF](https://www.gwern.net/docs/spaced-repetition/2004-brady.pdf)
- **2024 systematic review of contextual interference (Frontiers in Psychology).** Random practice was favoured in adults (SMD 0.54) and older adults (SMD 1.28). The effect was negligible in young participants (SMD 0.12). — [Frontiers 2024, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1377122](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1377122/full)
- **External vs internal focus of attention.**
  - Wulf (2013), "Attentional focus and motor learning: a review of 15 years", *Int Rev Sport Exerc Psychol* 6(1):77–104. An external focus (on the effect of the movement) improves performance and learning compared with an internal focus (on body movements), across populations. — [scinapse record](https://scinapse.io/papers/2044201858)
  - Chua, Jimenez-Diaz, Lewthwaite, Kim & Wulf (2021), *Psychological Bulletin*, meta-analysis of 73 performance studies and 40 learning studies. External focus beat internal focus: Hedges' g ≈ 0.26 for performance and ≈ 0.58 for retention/transfer. Age, health status and skill level did not moderate the effect. — [UNLV record](https://digitalscholarship.unlv.edu/kns_fac_articles/424); [PDF](https://peda.net/id/f0b68d288b0:file/download/9c596269dc5e3b461bdf57a54e0b26e0905025cc/Chua%202021%20SuperiorityofExternalAttentionalFocusforMotorPerformanceandLearning-SystematicReviewsandMeta-Analyses.pdf)
  - Counterpoints: a 2022 meta-analysis of immediate effects found a smaller positive effect with moderate-to-substantial heterogeneity and wide prediction intervals. The benefit may shrink when there is no tangible movement effect, such as a ball or club, to attend to. — [PMC10244639](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10244639/)
- **OPTIMAL theory (Wulf & Lewthwaite 2016), *Psychon Bull Rev* 23(5):1382–1414.** Three factors drive it: enhanced expectancies (for example positive feedback or a "liberal" success criterion), autonomy (self-controlled practice or choice) and an external focus. These factors reduce self-focus, strengthen goal–action coupling, and improve performance and learning. In follow-up studies, combining 2 or 3 factors gave additive benefits. — [UNLV record](https://oasis.library.unlv.edu/kns_fac_articles/123); [ProQuest](https://www.proquest.com/docview/1828126859)
- **Criticism of OPTIMAL.** A preprint review of 166 experiments found that only 21% measured motivation at all. The "Inconvenient findings" commentary challenges the theory's explanation of self-controlled practice benefits, which has replication problems. — [SportRxiv preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/download/390/806/748); [JEMS "Inconvenient findings"](https://www.scapps.org/jems/index.php/1/article/view/1796)

### Inferences
- Coaching-cue features should default to external-focus phrasing ("push the floor away", "reach toward the target"), not internal phrasing ("squeeze your glutes"). Treat this as a reasonable default, not a guaranteed effect: the effect is smaller in rehab contexts with no clear external target.
- A practice scheduler could let users toggle between blocked and random/variable scheduling. Blocked practice suits early acquisition and the cognitive stage; random practice suits later stages and adults.
- The OPTIMAL factors map onto UI choices: let the patient choose the exercise order (autonomy) and frame success criteria as achievable (expectancies).

### Gaps
- **Fitts & Posner (1967), *Human Performance*.** The classic three stages are cognitive, associative and autonomous. No source was retrieved this session, so this is from domain knowledge.
- **Dynamic systems theory** (Bernstein's degrees-of-freedom problem, self-organisation, attractors and control parameters; Kelso, Thelen) and **ecological / constraints-led approaches** (Newell 1986: organismic, task and environmental constraints). No primary source was retrieved.
- **Definitions (Shumway-Cook & Woollacott, domain knowledge).** Motor control is the ability to regulate or direct the mechanisms essential to movement. Motor learning is a set of processes associated with practice or experience that leads to relatively permanent changes in the capability for skilled action, inferred from retention and transfer. Verify the exact wording in the textbook.
- **Feedback scheduling** (knowledge of results vs knowledge of performance, reduced or faded frequency, the guidance hypothesis, self-controlled feedback; Schmidt & Lee). Not searched this session.

## Q2. Lumbopelvic / core motor control: TrA findings and their evolution, Luomajoki battery, ASLR, Cochrane MCE review

### Takeaway
The Hodges & Richardson findings (1996, 1998) of delayed transversus abdominis (TrA) feedforward onset in LBP launched "core stability" and TrA-specific training. That narrow interpretation has since been criticised (Lederman 2010) and reframed: Hodges & Tucker 2011 describe pain-related motor adaptation as protective and individual. The Cochrane review (Saragiotto 2016; 29 RCTs, n=2,431) found motor control exercise (MCE) better than minimal intervention but not clinically superior to other exercise. The Luomajoki movement-control battery is reliable (kappa roughly 0.24–0.71 inter-rater) and is a usable test battery for an app.

### Cited Findings
- **Hodges & Richardson 1996**, "Inefficient muscular stabilization of the lumbar spine associated with low back pain: a motor control evaluation of transversus abdominis", *Spine* 21:2640–50.
  - Design: 15 LBP patients vs 15 matched controls doing rapid shoulder flexion, abduction and extension to a visual stimulus.
  - Controls: TrA was invariably the first muscle active (feedforward).
  - LBP patients: TrA onset was significantly delayed in all directions.
  - Authors' reading: a motor-control deficit causing inefficient spinal stabilisation.
  — [QxMD record, doi:10.1097/00007632-199611150-00014](https://read.qxmd.com/doi/10.1097/00007632-199611150-00014)
- **Hodges & Richardson 1998**, *J Spinal Disord*. TrA EMG onset was also delayed with lower-limb movement in each direction in the LBP group. — [PubMed 9493770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9493770/)
- **Critiques of the TrA evidence.**
  - The delayed onset may reflect the lack of trunk rotation in the arm-raise task rather than a TrA-specific deficit.
  - Feedforward responses were not universal even in pain-free controls: 3 of 8 controls lacked feedforward responses in 70% of trials during bilateral arm raises.
  — [St Mary's University PDF "Transversus abdominis and core stability" (Lederman)](https://www.stmarys.ac.uk/document-library/pdfs/transversus-abdominis-and-core-stability.pdf)
- **Lederman (2010)**, "The myth of core stability", *J Bodyw Mov Ther* 14(1):84–98, doi:10.1016/j.jbmt.2009.08.001. A critical review that challenges three assumptions: that specific muscles (TrA) are more important for spinal stability, that weak abdominals cause back pain, and that strengthening trunk muscles reduces back pain. — [PubMed 20006294](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20006294/); [PDF](https://www.stmarys.ac.uk/document-library/pdfs/the-myth-of-core-stability.pdf)
  - FLAG for the report writer: "core stability" as a TrA-isolation or "weak core causes LBP" claim is outdated or overstated.
- **Hodges & Tucker (2011)**, "Moving differently in pain: a new theory to explain the adaptation to pain", *Pain* 152(3 Suppl):S90–S98, doi:10.1016/j.pain.2010.10.020.
  - Pain causes redistribution of activity within and between muscles. It changes mechanical behaviour, for example by increasing stiffness or "splinting".
  - This is protective in the short term but may have long-term consequences.
  - It replaces the simple "inhibition" and "pain-spasm-pain" models.
  — [UQ publication list](https://about.uq.edu.au/experts/1050?page=19). Content was confirmed only through secondary summaries; check the primary article.
- **Luomajoki et al. 2007**, *BMC Musculoskelet Disord* 8:90, doi:10.1186/1471-2474-8-90. Reliability of 10 active movement-control tests.
  - Design: 4 physiotherapists rated videos of 27 non-specific LBP patients and 13 people without back pain, scoring each test correct or incorrect.
  - Inter-rater: kappa 0.24–0.71. Five of 10 tests had kappa above 0.6, four were 0.4–0.6 and one was below 0.4.
  - Intra-rater: five tests had kappa above 0.80, four were 0.6–0.8 and one was 0.51.
  - Percentage agreement: 65–97.5%.
  — [PMC2164955](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2164955); [PDF](https://bmcmusculoskeletdisord.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1471-2474-8-90)
- **Luomajoki et al. 2008**, *BMC Musculoskelet Disord* (PMC2635372). Used a six-test subset to compare LBP patients with healthy controls (N=210). The six tests usually cited from this paper are: (1) waiter's bow, (2) pelvic tilt (dorsal/backward), (3) one-leg stance, (4) sitting knee extension, (5) rocking forward/backward in 4-point kneeling, and (6) prone knee bend. — [PMC2635372](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2635372); [Physiotutors summary](https://www.physiotutors.com/wiki/motor-control-dysfunction-screening/). The full text was blocked by a reCAPTCHA, so check the test list and statistics against the primary paper.
- **Active straight leg raise (ASLR).**
  - Scoring: the patient rates each leg 0 ("not difficult at all") to 5 ("unable to do"), and the two sides are summed for a 0–10 score.
  - Proposed cut-off: 0 = negative, 1–10 = positive.
  - Test-retest reliability (1 week): ICC 0.83, attributed to the 2002 Mens/Vleeming paper in *Acta Obstet Gynecol Scand*, with n=50 for reliability and n=200 for sensitivity. — [Medbridge summary](https://medbridge.com/exam-tests/test/42) (secondary).
  - Inter-rater reliability in women with low-back and pelvic pain: kappa 0.87, sensitivity 71%, specificity 91%. — [J Rehabil Med](https://medicaljournals.se/jrm/content/html/10.2340/16501977-1213)
  - Mens et al. 2010 (*Spine*, n=21 parous women): subjective ASLR scores correlate with objectively measured forces. — [PubMed 20075777](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/20075777)
- **Cochrane review: Saragiotto et al. 2016**, "Motor control exercise for chronic non-specific low-back pain", CD012004, doi:10.1002/14651858.CD012004. Also published in *Spine* in 2016.
  - Scope: 29 RCTs, n=2,431.
  - vs minimal intervention: MCE improved pain and disability.
  - vs other exercise: no clinically important difference in pain or disability at short-, intermediate- or long-term follow-up. For example, 11 trials (n=794) showed no clinically important effect on short-term disability.
  - Authors' recommendation: choose the exercise based on patient and therapist preference, cost and availability.
  — [Cochrane Library](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD012004/information); [George Institute](https://georgeinstitute.org/publications/motor-control-exercise-for-chronic-non-specific-low-back-pain); [IWH summary](https://iwh.on.ca/node/184)

### Inferences
- An app's LBP motor-control module can implement the six-test Luomajoki battery as binary pass/fail items with a total score of 0–6. Use it as an impairment descriptor, not a diagnosis or a predictor of outcome.
- The UI should not present MCE as superior to general exercise for chronic LBP, and should not frame TrA "drawing-in" as necessary. The evidence supports MCE as one option among equivalent exercise choices.
- The ASLR maps cleanly to a 0–10 numeric input (sum of two 0–5 sliders) with a positive flag at ≥1.

### Gaps
- **Luomajoki 2008 numbers and scoring (domain knowledge, not verified).** Recalled: 108 LBP vs 102 controls; mean positive tests about 2.2 vs 0.75 of 6; effect size d ≈ 1.18; p < .001. Also recalled: a "≥2 positive tests" heuristic is often cited as a clinical flag, which is a secondary-literature convention.
  - Recalled per-test failure criteria: waiter's bow = lumbar flexion before about 50–70° hip flexion; dorsal pelvic tilt = no isolated pelvic tilt; one-leg stance = lateral shift difference >2 cm side-to-side; sitting knee extension = lumbar flexion during knee extension; rocking = lumbar flexion/extension during rocking (hip flexion of 60–120°); prone knee bend = lumbar extension/rotation before 90° knee flexion.
  - Verify all of these against the full text.
- **Later Hodges work on TrA timing** (for example 2003 studies; ultrasound imaging of TrA thickness) and later RCTs such as Ferreira 2007 and Costa 2009. Not retrieved.
- **Newer MCE vs general-exercise network meta-analyses.** Hayden et al. 2021, the Cochrane exercise update and the *BJSM* network meta-analysis, found small between-type differences, with Pilates and MCE ranking well but low certainty. Not retrieved, so this is from domain knowledge.
- **Comerford & Mottram "Kinetic Control" (2012).** The concepts of "uncontrolled movement" (UCM) and the Kinetic Control rating were not searched; no source.

## Q3. Lower-limb dynamic control, scapular dyskinesis, cervical motor control

### Takeaway
Visual rating of the single-leg squat, step-down and drop vertical jump has only moderate reliability (κ roughly 0.4–0.8, depending on scale and rater). Hewett 2005 linked dynamic knee valgus (DKV) to ACL injury, but Krosshaug 2016 (n=710) found the vertical drop jump cannot predict ACL injury. These tests should be treated as movement-quality descriptors, not injury-risk screens. Scapular dyskinesis is common but not clearly causal (2013 consensus). The McClure scapular dyskinesis test (SDT) has moderate reliability. The craniocervical flexion test (CCFT) uses five 2-mmHg stages from 20 to 30 mmHg.

### Cited Findings
- **Hewett et al. 2005**, *AJSM* 33(4):492–501, doi:10.1177/0363546504269591. Prospective study: 9 female athletes who sustained ACL injury had about 8° more dynamic valgus and higher knee abduction moments at drop-jump landing than 196 uninjured athletes. — [IJSPT review](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/128587-are-we-overlooking-anatomical-contributions-to-dynamic-knee-valgus)
- **Krosshaug et al. 2016**, "The vertical drop jump is a poor screening test for ACL injuries in female elite soccer and handball players: a prospective cohort study of 710 athletes", *AJSM* 44(4):874–883, doi:10.1177/0363546515625048.
  - Data: 782 players tested with 3D motion analysis between 2007 and 2014; 710 analysed; 42 new non-contact ACL injuries.
  - Conclusion: VDJ tests cannot predict ACL injury.
  - A 2017 corrigendum fixed the medial knee displacement calculation. After correction there was still no association, and the main conclusion stands.
  — [NIH Brage full text](https://nih.brage.unit.no/nih-xmlui/bitstream/handle/11250/2394616/KrosshaugAmJSportMed2016.pdf?sequence=1); [Corrigendum](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/reader/10.1177/0363546517716155)
- **Related "Kiss goodbye to the kissing knees" study (Nilstad/Krosshaug group).** In 880 elite female athletes, frontal-plane knee motion on visually rated single-leg squat and VDJ was a weak predictor of non-contact ACL injury. — [Lingnan record](https://scholars.ln.edu.hk/en/publications/kiss-goodbye-to-the-kissing-knees-no-association-between-frontal-/). A 2025 review describes the evidence linking DKV to injury as contradictory. — [PMC11788082](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11788082)
  - FLAG: "knee valgus = ACL risk" as a screening claim is contested or outdated for prediction. DKV is still a reasonable target for training and technique cueing.
- **Single-leg squat (Crossley et al. 2011, *AJSM*).**
  - A consensus panel of 5 clinicians rated 34 asymptomatic people as good, fair or poor on criteria covering overall impression, trunk posture, pelvis "in space", hip and knee.
  - Good performers had earlier gluteus medius onset and greater hip abduction and lateral trunk strength.
  - A secondary table reports inter- and intra-rater κ of 0.6–0.8 (73–87% agreement).
  — [Univ. Melbourne record](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/332507-performance-on-the-single-leg-squat-task-indicates-hip-abductor-muscle-function)
- **Rating scale granularity (2025 study).**
  - Two categories: inter-rater κ 0.60, intra-rater κ 0.60.
  - Three categories: inter-rater κ 0.61, intra-rater κ 0.35.
  - Four categories: inter-rater κ 0.33, intra-rater κ 0.20.
  - The authors recommend a three-category scale. Given the low three-category intra-rater value, read this as a trade-off between detail and reliability.
  — [PMC12535228](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12535228/)
- **Single-leg squat kinematic rating.** Physiotherapists' inter-rater ICC was 0.71 and intra-rater ICC 0.81; students' were 0.60 and 0.71. — [PMC6044589](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6044589)
- **Lateral step-down in patellofemoral pain (PFP).** Video rating of movement quality in 22 PFP participants (Mansfield 2021) gave inter- and intra-rater κ of 0.40–0.65. Rater experience made no difference. — [ISBS/NMU](https://commons.nmu.edu/isbs/vol42/iss1/46) (identification is from a search snippet; verify the link). Piva et al. 2006 (*BMC MSK Disord*, PMC1557500) studied reliability of PFP impairment measures in 30 patients. — [PMC1557500](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1557500)
- **Scapular dyskinesis consensus: Kibler et al. 2013**, "Clinical implications of scapular dyskinesis in shoulder injury: the 2013 consensus statement from the 'scapular summit'", *BJSM* 47:877–885. Co-authors include McClure, Ludewig, Michener, Bak and Sciascia.
  - Dyskinesis is present in a high proportion of shoulder injuries, but its exact causal role is not defined. It is best viewed as a "potential impairment".
  - A reliable observational clinical evaluation method is available.
  - Scapula-focused rehabilitation can be effective within comprehensive shoulder rehabilitation.
  — [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/47/14/877); [UMN record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/clinical-implications-of-scapular-dyskinesis-in-shoulder-injury-t/)
- **McClure SDT, part 1: reliability (2009, *J Athl Train* 44(2):160)**.
  - Protocol: 5 repetitions each of bilateral weighted shoulder flexion and frontal-plane abduction, viewed from behind. Weights are 1.4 kg (3 lb) if body mass is under 68.1 kg and 2.3 kg (5 lb) if 68.1 kg or more.
  - Rating: normal, subtle or obvious dyskinesis.
  - Reliability: 75–82% agreement, weighted κ 0.48–0.61.
  — [JAT PDF](https://nata.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/attr/44/2/article-p160.pdf); [SLUHN record](https://crin.sluhn.org/crms_ap/9)
- **McClure SDT, part 2: validity.** "Obvious" dyskinesis corresponded to distinct 3D scapular motion changes but was not related to shoulder symptoms in overhead athletes. — [SLUHN part 2](https://crin.sluhn.org/cpcr_ap/7)
- **Craniocervical flexion test (Jull).**
  - Setup: supine, with a pressure biofeedback unit under the neck at a 20 mmHg baseline. The patient does a nodding (craniocervical flexion) action in five stages of +2 mmHg each (22, 24, 26, 28, 30), without superficial muscle substitution.
  - Protocols differ. The activation score is the highest stage held for about 2–3 s. Other versions use 10-s holds.
  - Endurance stage: 10 × 10-s holds.
  — [Physiotutors CCFT](https://www.physiotutors.com/wiki/craniocervical-flexion-test/); [JNER 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12984-024-01438-2)
- **CCFT reliability evidence is thin.** One estimate is κ = 0.72 from only 10 asymptomatic subjects. A systematic-review protocol on CCFT measurement properties exists. — [BMJ Open protocol](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/8/2/e019486); [PMC5855340](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5855340)
- **Jull et al. 2005, JOSPT.** "Performance of the craniocervical flexion test in subjects with and without chronic neck pain". — [Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/jospt/pdf/00001218-200509000-00003~performance-of-the-craniocervical-flexion-test-in-subjects)

### Inferences
- In the app, store visual-rating tests (single-leg squat, step-down, SDT, Luomajoki) on 2- or 3-level ordinal scales. Use 2 levels when inter-session reliability matters most.
- Do not output an "ACL injury risk" score from DKV or the drop vertical jump. Label results as "movement quality" or "dynamic knee control".
- For scapular dyskinesis, use SDT categories (normal, subtle, obvious) as an impairment descriptor that is not directly tied to symptoms.

### Gaps
- **Step-down test scoring.** The Piva 2006 lateral step-down quality score (0–6 based on arm strategy, trunk, pelvis, knee position and balance; 0–1 good, 2–3 moderate, ≥4 poor) is recalled from domain knowledge and was not verified. Piva 2006's PMC paper may not include it.
- **Drop vertical jump clinical tools** (Landing Error Scoring System, LESS) were not searched.
- **Kibler's observational "yes/no" classification and the scapular assistance and retraction tests** were not retrieved.
- **CCFT normative cut-offs** were not verified. Recalled: healthy people usually reach 26–30 mmHg, and neck-pain patients often fail below 26 mmHg.

## Q4. Proprioception and neuromuscular control: JPS testing and injury-prevention programmes

### Takeaway
Cervical joint position error testing (Revel 1991) discriminates neck-pain patients from controls (Se 86%, Sp 93%), but its protocols are not standardised. Neuromuscular training (NMT) programmes cut ACL injuries by about half: Webster & Hewett 2018, an umbrella meta-analysis, found OR 0.50 (95% CI 0.41–0.59). FIFA 11+ cut overall football injuries by about 39% (Thorborg 2017, IRR 0.61). The effect depends on adherence, at least 2–3 sessions per week.

### Cited Findings
- **Revel, Andre-Deshays & Minguet 1991**, "Cervicocephalic kinesthetic sensibility in patients with cervical pain", *Arch Phys Med Rehabil* 72(5):288–291.
  - Test: head relocation to a neutral reference after active rotation, with error measured in degrees.
  - Accuracy: sensitivity 86%, specificity 93%.
  — [Chiro.org PDF](https://chiro.org/Graphics_Box_RESEARCH/Cervicocephalic_Kinesthetic_Sensibility.pdf); [SRAlab Cervical JPE test](https://www.sralab.org/rehabilitation-measures/cervical-joint-position-error-test)
- **No consensus on method.** There is no agreement on how to perform head-repositioning tests or how to dichotomise results. Some chronic non-traumatic neck-pain samples show no deficit. — [Chiropr Osteopat 2009, doi:10.1186/1746-1340-17-6](https://chiromt.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1746-1340-17-6)
- **Webster & Hewett 2018**, *J Orthop Res*, doi:10.1002/jor.24043. A meta-analysis of 8 meta-analyses, 6 of them female-only. ACL injury reduction programmes cut all ACL injuries by 50% (OR 0.50, 95% CI 0.41–0.59, I² = 15%). A second figure of OR 0.33 (a 67% reduction) appeared, likely for a subgroup such as non-contact ACL injuries or females; the subgroup was not confirmed. — [Abstract mirror](https://mbevidencias.apm.org.br/vufind/Record/openalex-W2807861602/Description?lng=en)
- **Earlier team-sport meta-analysis (Journal of Athletic Training).** Non-contact ACL RR 0.30. Sensitivity analyses: all studies RR 0.41 (0.27–0.63); RCTs only RR 0.52 (0.34–0.80). — [JAT 52(12):1171 PDF](https://nata.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/attr/52/12/article-p1171.pdf); [ECU record](https://thescholarship.ecu.edu/items/04f1e753-29cb-423b-b5a7-b1d3438257ed/full)
- **Thorborg et al. 2017**, *BJSM* 51(7):562, a meta-analysis of RCTs of FIFA 11 and 11+.
  - Overall IRR 0.75 (0.57–0.98).
  - FIFA 11+ alone (4 RCTs): IRR 0.61 (0.48–0.77), a 39% injury reduction.
  - The original FIFA 11 alone: IRR 0.99, no effect.
  — [PubMed 28087568](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28087568/)
- **FIFA 11+ in youth (systematic review of 10 studies).** ACL risk reductions of 30–77%. Effectiveness depends heavily on coach adherence, supervision quality and frequency of at least 2–3 sessions per week. — [JSKK review](https://journals.itb.ac.id/index.php/jskk/article/view/26363)
- **2025 soccer meta-analysis (11 studies, more than 10,000 players).** NMT, especially FIFA 11+, reduced injuries compared with standard warm-ups, with a larger ACL benefit in females. — [PMC11899896](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11899896/)
- **PEP (Prevent injury and Enhance Performance), Santa Monica Sports Medicine Foundation; Mandelbaum et al. 2005, AJSM.**
  - Population: 14–18-year-old female club soccer players.
  - Format: a warm-up with running, stretching, strengthening, plyometrics and agility, plus landing technique ("soft landing on bent knee and hip").
  - Reported ACL reduction: 88% in year 1 and 74% in year 2, per news coverage. Another summary reports 83% and 72%, so the figures conflict; verify against the primary paper.
  - Gilchrist et al. 2008 (*AJSM*): an RCT in NCAA Division I women's soccer found PEP appeared to reduce ACL injury risk.
  — [ClinicalTrials NCT00140270](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00140270); [Cochrane S4BE review of Gilchrist 2008](https://s4be.cochrane.org/blog/2014/03/11/neuromuscular-strength-training-to-prevent-acl-injury-in-ncaa-womens-soccer-players-article-review); [LA84 PEP study](https://la84.org/?p=3550)

### Inferences
- A useful product feature is an adherence tracker (sessions per week, with a target of at least 2–3) for an injury-prevention warm-up module. The evidence makes adherence the main moderator of effect.
- The roughly 50% ACL reduction figure is defensible if cited to Webster & Hewett 2018, OR 0.50. The PEP 88% figure comes from a non-randomised early study and should not be shown as the expected effect.
- JPS testing can be entered as absolute error in degrees, averaged over trials, with a caveat that cut-offs are not standardised.

### Gaps
- **The common "4.5° cervical JPE" threshold** (often attributed to Revel 1991 or Treleaven) was not verified this session.
- **Knee and ankle JPS protocols** (for example, active reproduction of target knee angles with absolute and constant error) and their normative values were not searched.
- **IOC consensus statements on injury prevention** (for example, the 2008 IOC consensus on non-contact ACL injury) were not retrieved.
- **Mandelbaum 2005 exact citation** (AJSM 33(7):1003–1010, recalled) and its exact effect figures were not verified from the primary source.

## Q5. Exercise progression frameworks for motor control

### Takeaway
No single validated, evidence-graded progression algorithm for motor-control exercise was found this session. The common clinical heuristic is static → dynamic, stable → unstable, slow → fast, simple → complex, low → high load, and closed → open environment. It is consistent with the motor-learning findings above (Fitts & Posner stages; blocked → random/variable practice; internal → external focus). It should be presented as expert-consensus or textbook guidance, not as RCT-proven.

### Cited Findings
- **Contextual interference evidence supports progressing from blocked to random/variable practice as skill develops.** Random practice hurts acquisition but helps retention and transfer, and the effect is stronger in adults and older adults. — [Brady 2004](https://www.gwern.net/docs/spaced-repetition/2004-brady.pdf); [Frontiers 2024](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1377122/full)
- **Schmidt's schema theory supports variable practice** to build generalisable parameter rules, though the evidence is mixed. — [Schmidt summary PDF](https://grants.hhp.uh.edu/clayne/4315videos/KIN4315vid/6schmidt.pdf); [Sherwood & Lee 2003](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4008365)
- **Shumway-Cook & Woollacott's task-oriented approach** frames progression as manipulating individual, task and environment constraints toward functional tasks. — [ProQuest record](https://proquest.com/scholarly-journals/motor-control-theory-practical-applications-ed-2/docview/223115309/se-2)
- **Injury-prevention programmes build in progression.** FIFA 11+ has three difficulty levels per exercise, and PEP progresses through plyometrics and agility. Effect depends on dose (at least 2–3 sessions per week). — [JSKK FIFA 11+ review](https://journals.itb.ac.id/index.php/jskk/article/view/26363); [Thorborg 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28087568/)
- **The Saragiotto 2016 Cochrane MCE protocol** commonly progressed from isolated deep-muscle activation to integration into functional tasks. It was no better than other exercise, which argues against making "isolation first" a mandatory gate. — [Cochrane CD012004](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD012004/information)

### Inferences
- Suggested data model for app progression logic:
  - Dimensions: base of support (bilateral → split → single-leg); surface (stable → unstable); speed or tempo (slow controlled, e.g. 3-1-3, → fast/ballistic); plane (sagittal → frontal/transverse); load (bodyweight → external); cognitive or dual-task (none → dual-task); environment (closed/predictable → open/reactive); practice schedule (blocked → random); feedback (frequent → faded/self-controlled); cue focus (external by default).
  - Advance one dimension at a time when the test criterion is met, for example a "good" rating on a 2–3-level scale for 2 consecutive sessions without symptom increase. These rules are clinical heuristics, not evidence-derived thresholds.
- Use the Luomajoki tests, single-leg squat and step-down ratings as "gates": a pass unlocks the next level. This turns the assessment battery into progression logic.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed, validated motor-control progression algorithm was found. Comerford & Mottram's *Kinetic Control* (2012) rating of uncontrolled movement and the O'Sullivan "cognitive functional therapy" progression were not retrieved.
- No evidence was found for specific tempo prescriptions (e.g. 3-1-3) for motor-control outcomes. These come from the strength-training literature and are not motor-control-specific.
- The evidence base for unstable-surface training (stable → unstable) as superior for motor control or injury prevention was not searched. Treat it as a heuristic.
