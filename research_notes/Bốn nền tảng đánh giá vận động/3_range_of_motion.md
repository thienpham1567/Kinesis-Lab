# Range of Motion (ROM) — Biên độ vận động — in PT & rehabilitation

Research notes compiled 2026-10-16. Scope: normative ROM values, definitions (AROM/PROM, end-feel, capsular pattern, hypermobility), measurement methods and their reliability, ROM-related clinical tests, functional ROM, and stretching dose. Out of scope (other researchers cover these): movement screens, L/R symmetry indices, motor control.

## 1. Normative active ROM values per joint/movement, where sources disagree, and age/sex effects

### Takeaway
No single "normal" ROM exists. The AAOS (1965), the AMA Guides and textbook tables (Norkin & White, Kendall, Magee) give rounded consensus figures that differ by 10–20° for some movements, especially wrist, cervical rotation, knee and hip flexion. Large measured datasets such as CDC/Soucie (n>600, ages 2–69) show that true means are often lower than the AAOS figures and fall steadily with age. Females are consistently more mobile than males by about 2–7°. An app should store values per source, flag against age- and sex-specific norms where they exist (Soucie), and fall back on side-to-side comparison where they don't.

### Inferences
- The Soucie data show the size of the age effect. From ages 20–44 to 45–69, males lose about 4–5° of knee flexion, about 5° of shoulder flexion and about 4° of hip extension. Children aged 2–8 have the largest ROM, especially DF, PF and hip extension. An app's "normal" flag should be age-banded and sex-specific wherever Soucie covers the joint.
- Females exceed males in almost every Soucie cell. The gap is largest for ankle PF (about 7–11°), elbow hyperextension (about 4–5°) and supination (about 5°).
- AAOS figures for hip extension (30°) and ankle DF (20°) are higher than the measured adult means (about 17° and about 13°). If the app uses AAOS as a single cutoff, it will flag many healthy adults as limited. A sensible rule: flag when a value is below the Soucie lower 95% CI for that age/sex, or more than 10° below the AAOS figure, and always show the contralateral side.
- The Soucie CIs are confidence intervals of the mean, not reference ranges (±2 SD). For flags, compute a reference range (for example, the 2.5th–97.5th percentile) from the downloadable raw dataset. Do not use the CIs as a normal range.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the Norkin & White appendix tables (paywalled), the Kendall values or the AAOS 1965 handbook directly. The bracketed values are textbook figures that have not been verified.
- Soucie does not cover shoulder abduction/rotation, wrist, spine, hip abduction/rotation or subtalar motion. Those joints need other normative studies (for example, Boone & Azen 1979 and Roaas & Andersson 1982), which I did not retrieve.
- I did not check whether the latest AMA Guides edition (AMA Guides Sixth 2021/2022 digital update) changed any ROM figures.

## 2. Definitions: AROM vs PROM, end-feel (Cyriax), capsular patterns, hypo-/hypermobility (Beighton, 2017 criteria)

### Takeaway
Capsular patterns are well documented in textbooks (Cyriax, Magee), but their diagnostic validity is weak, especially for hip OA. Hypermobility is graded with the 9-point Beighton score. Since 2017 the cutoff for generalized joint hypermobility (GJH) is age-dependent: ≥6 before puberty, ≥5 from puberty to age 50, ≥4 over 50. The older single cutoff of ≥4 should not be hard-coded.

### Cited Findings
- **Capsular patterns** (Cyriax, per Magee 2014, most restricted motion listed first) — [PhysicalTherapyWeb Cyriax table](https://physicaltherapyweb.com/joint-capsular-patterns/):
  - Cervical spine: side flexion and rotation equally limited, then extension.
  - Glenohumeral: lateral (external) rotation > abduction > medial rotation.
  - Elbow (humeroulnar): flexion > extension.
  - Proximal radioulnar: supination > pronation. Distal radioulnar: pain at the extremes of rotation.
  - Wrist: flexion and extension equally limited.
  - Hip: flexion, abduction, medial rotation (order varies by stage).
  - Knee: flexion > extension.
  - Talocrural: plantarflexion > dorsiflexion.
  - Subtalar: limitation of varus.
  - Thoracic/lumbar spine: side flexion and rotation equally limited, then extension.
  - First MTP: extension > flexion.
  - A capsular restriction is usually accompanied by a firm or hard end-feel.
- **Validity of the capsular pattern is weak.** In Bijl et al. 1998 (*Scand J Rheumatol* 27(5):347–351; n=200), hip OA showed no distinct capsular pattern. Knee OA showed restricted flexion and extension only in some subgroups. The authors concluded that the capsular pattern is not a valid diagnostic test for hip or knee OA — [NIVEL record](https://www.nivel.nl/en/publicatie/validity-cyriaxs-concept-capsular-pattern-diagnosis-osteoarthritis-hip-andor-knee); [UMC Utrecht](https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/validity-of-cyriaxs-concept-of-capsular-pattern-for-the-diagnosis/).
- In a knee OA study (n=30), only 13.3% of patients showed a knee capsular pattern. The authors questioned the capsular pattern as a diagnostic tool. Earlier work by Hayes, Petersen & Falconer (1994, *Phys Ther*) also questioned Cyriax's passive motion tests in knee OA — [IJPOT](https://www.i-scholar.in/index.php/ijpot/article/view/47179/0).
- **Beighton cutoffs (2017 International EDS Consortium; Malfait et al. 2017, *Am J Med Genet C*):** GJH is defined as a Beighton score ≥6 for prepubertal children and adolescents, ≥5 for pubertal men and women up to age 50, and ≥4 for those over 50 — [EDS Society 2017 classification](https://www.ehlers-danlos.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/2017-EDS-Classification_March_2017.pdf); [PMC6744937](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6744937).
- If the Beighton score is 1 point below the age/sex cutoff because of acquired joint limitation (past surgery, amputation and similar), GJH can still be diagnosed when the 5-Point Questionnaire (5PQ) is positive (≥2 items) — [PMC6744937](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6744937).
- A diagnosis of hEDS requires Criterion 1 (GJH), Criterion 2 and Criterion 3 together — [EDS Society hEDS vs HSD](https://www.ehlers-danlos.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/hEDSvHSD.pdf).
- The age adjustment exists because joint mobility falls with age. The score is "all or none" and cannot grade the degree of hypermobility. In the past, ≥4 was often used for adults, and some sources give ≥5 as Beighton's original cutoff — [PMC6744937](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6744937); [BMJ Case Reports](https://bibliotheek.ehb.be:2635/content/bmjcr/15/11/e252690.full.pdf).

### Inferences
- Data model for Beighton: 9 binary items: little-finger MCP passive extension >90° (L/R), thumb to forearm (L/R), elbow hyperextension >10° (L/R), knee hyperextension >10° (L/R), and palms flat on the floor with knees straight (1). The GJH flag then depends on age and pubertal status, plus the optional 5PQ rule. (The item definitions are standard Beighton content. I did not re-verify the wording this session; check against Malfait 2017.)
- Because the capsular-pattern evidence is weak, the app can show capsular patterns as educational hints ("gợi ý"), not as diagnostic rules.

### Gaps
- Cyriax end-feel definitions were not retrieved from a primary source this session. The standard textbook content is listed here for the writer to verify: normal end-feels are bone-to-bone (hard, e.g. elbow extension), soft-tissue approximation (e.g. knee flexion) and tissue stretch (firm, e.g. shoulder ER). Pathological end-feels are early muscle spasm, late muscle spasm, hard capsular, soft capsular, bone-to-bone (abnormal), empty (pain stops motion before resistance) and springy block (internal derangement). Magee 2014 and Cyriax 1982 are the sources to cite.
- I found no inter-rater reliability data for end-feel judgments this session. Older literature reports only modest kappa values, which I could not verify.
- I did not confirm definitions of AROM and PROM against a source this session. Standard definitions: AROM is motion produced by the patient's own muscle contraction, and PROM is motion produced by an external force with the muscles relaxed. PROM is normally slightly larger than AROM. Norkin & White is the source to cite.
- Hypomobility has no formal numeric cutoff. In practice it is defined relative to norms or the contralateral side.

## 3. Measurement methods (goniometer, inclinometer, smartphone, motion capture, markerless video) and their reliability (ICC, SEM, MDC)

### Takeaway
Universal goniometry is the clinical reference standard. Its reliability is good within the same rater and lower between raters, and it depends on the joint (knee better than shoulder or hip). Smartphone apps reach ICCs comparable to the goniometer for most peripheral joints, but are weak for spinal rotation. Markerless pose estimation is promising for sagittal knee and hip motion but still shows errors of about 4–12° and underestimates end-range. Typical knee goniometry SEM is about 1–3.5° (MDC about 2.5–8°). Shoulder SEM ranges from about 2° to 14°.

### Cited Findings
**Universal goniometer**
- Classic review: Gajdosik RL, Bohannon RW. "Clinical measurement of range of motion: review of goniometry emphasizing reliability and validity." *Phys Ther* 1987;67(12):1867–1872, doi:10.1093/ptj/67.12.1867. Per secondary citation, it concluded that hip motions can be measured reliably if procedures are tightly controlled — [Campbell Univ repository](https://cufind.campbell.edu/physical_therapy/306); [PMC4842428](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4842428).
- Riddle, Rothstein & Lamb 1987 (*Phys Ther*): shoulder PROM goniometry was highly reliable within the same therapist. Between-tester reliability depended on the motion — [PubMed 3575423](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3575423/).
- Knee, in 60 patients with knee OA or TKR (J Knee Surg 2017;30(6):577–584): universal goniometer SEM 3.41° (flexion) and 1.62° (extension). The i-Goni smartphone app had SEM 2.72° and 1.18°, with MDC 6.3° (flexion) and 2.72° (extension) — [Thieme, doi:10.1055/s-0036-1593877](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/s-0036-1593877).
- Healthy adults, active knee ROM: smallest detectable change ranged from 1.6° (extension) to 2.3° (flexion). Flexion was more reliable than extension — [Univ. Ljubljana repository](https://repozitorij.uni-lj.si/IzpisGradiva.php?id=131247).
- Knee rotational laxity by double goniometry: ICC 0.66–0.86, SEM 1.4–2.0°, MDC 2.5–3.8° — [hrcak.srce.hr](https://hrcak.srce.hr/en/337590).

**Smartphone apps (systematic review)**
- Keogh JWL et al. "Reliability and validity of clinically accessible smartphone applications to measure joint range of motion: A systematic review." *PLoS One* 2019;14(5):e0215806. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0215806. 37 studies in the body of the paper; the abstract mentions 25, an internal inconsistency — [PMC6505893](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6505893/). Approximate ICC ranges by joint:
  - Knee: intra-rater 0.83–0.99, inter-rater 0.89–0.99.
  - Shoulder: intra-rater 0.72–0.99, inter-rater 0.28–0.99.
  - Wrist: intra-rater 0.77–0.99, inter-rater 0.72–0.92.
  - Elbow: intra-rater 0.95–0.98, inter-rater 0.84–0.96.
  - Ankle: intra-rater 0.81–0.98, inter-rater 0.76–0.96.
  - Hip: intra-rater 0.63–0.94 (one study).
  - Spine: inter-rater 0.07–0.99, with rotation weakest (trunk rotation 0.07; cervical rotation 0.53).
- SEM/MDC examples in Keogh: thoracic rotation SEM 2.2–4.4° (limits of agreement about 25°). Lumbar flexion/extension SEM 2.1–3.0°. Cervical flexion MDC 6.9°. Wrist SEM 0.9–2.2° (MDC 2.6–6.2°). Knee flexion SEM 2.72° (MDC90 6.3°). Shoulder SEM 1.9–13.8°. Ankle SEM 1.4° — [PMC6505893](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6505893/).
- Keogh conclusions: relative reliability (ICC) is stronger than absolute (SEM, MDC, limits of agreement). Do not switch devices (goniometer ↔ app) within one patient over time. Only 5 studies used 3D motion capture as the criterion. Software updates may change performance — [PMC6505893](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6505893/).
- Smartphone measurement of SLR, ankle DF and hip IR: intra-rater ICC 0.668–0.939, inter-rater ICC 0.701–0.936. SLR and hip IR showed fixation errors between raters — [Physical Education of Students journal](https://sportedu.org.ua/index.php/PES/article/view/1449).

**Inclinometer**
- The AMA Guides use (dual) inclinometry for the spine and require 3 consecutive readings within 5° or 10% — see section 1. The weight-bearing lunge (section 4) uses an inclinometer on the tibia (inter-rater ICC 0.97) — [Bennell 1998 via SWU repository](https://ir.swu.ac.th/entities/publication/59be547d-8f4e-434b-a2f6-708fdd0fbaa8).

**Markerless video / pose estimation**
- No pooled systematic review was found. Individual studies:
  - Fingers: MediaPipe in lateral view had RMSE 4.22° against an electrogoniometer. OpenPose in frontal view performed poorly — [PMC11858933](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11858933/).
  - 2D OpenPose, gait: sagittal knee and hip angles on the camera side were near marker-based error levels. The occluded side was worse. Ankle limits of agreement were about ±12°, too wide for clinical use — [PMC10635560](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10635560/).
  - Camera viewing angle introduces significant bias in OpenPose joint angles — [TUM](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/influence-of-the-camera-viewing-angle-on-openpose-validity-in-mot/).
  - Upper limb, 2026: ICC >0.91 for the elbow and >0.94 for the shoulder in full-ROM trials, but maximum flexion was systematically underestimated, especially at the shoulder — [MDPI Sensors 25(4):1079](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/4/1079) (as summarized in search results; the year attribution is uncertain).
  - Running: correlations with the reference were high, but maximum flexion and extension were predicted poorly — [IJESAB](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/ijesab/vol14/iss2/179).

### Inferences
- For "real change" flags in progress tracking, use MDC95 = SEM × 1.96 × √2 (about 2.77 × SEM). Suggested defaults: knee flexion about 6° (UG SEM 3.4° gives about 9°), knee extension about 3–5°, wrist about 3–6°, and shoulder movement-specific, often ≥10°. Store a per-movement MDC setting with these as defaults.
- Store the instrument type (goniometer, inclinometer, app, video) with every measurement, and warn when it changes between sessions (per Keogh).
- Markerless video in 2026 is suitable for screening or trend tracking of sagittal-plane knee/hip/elbow motion with a camera perpendicular to the movement plane. It is not suitable for end-range clinical decisions or for ankle and rotation measurements.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve detailed standard goniometer positioning (axis, stationary and moving arm landmarks per joint). This is Norkin & White content and needs to be pulled from the textbook. Examples: knee axis at the lateral femoral epicondyle, stationary arm toward the greater trochanter, moving arm toward the lateral malleolus.
- I found no systematic review with pooled SEM/MDC for universal goniometry across all joints.
- I found no meta-analysis of 3D marker-based or IMU accuracy for clinical ROM this session.

## 4. Key ROM/flexibility clinical tests (Thomas, SLR/90-90, weight-bearing lunge, sit-and-reach, Apley, GIRD/total arc)

### Takeaway
Several tests rest on cutoffs with weak evidence. The <10 cm knee-to-wall cutoff and the 18–20° GIRD threshold are clinical conventions, not consensus values. Reliability is excellent for the weight-bearing lunge and good for the AKE/90-90 test. The modified Thomas test is unreliable with a goniometer unless pelvic tilt is controlled.

### Cited Findings
- **Weight-bearing lunge / knee-to-wall:** Bennell et al. 1998 (*Aust J Physiother* 44(3):175–180), 13 healthy subjects and 4 raters. Intra-rater ICC 0.97–0.98. Inter-rater ICC 0.97 (tibial angle by inclinometer) and 0.99 (toe-to-wall distance) — [SWU repository](https://ir.swu.ac.th/entities/publication/59be547d-8f4e-434b-a2f6-708fdd0fbaa8).
- Modified lunge (Grafton & O'Shea): intra-rater ICC 0.98–0.99, SEM 0.4 cm. Across studies, intra-rater ICC ranged 0.65–0.99 and inter-rater 0.80–0.99 — [SHU repository](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/10374/1/Simon_OShea__Kate_Grafton_Manual_Therapy_Reliability_Lunge_Test_submission_.pdf).
- A result below 10 cm indicates restricted DF in most people, but tall people can reach 10 cm through tibia length alone (false negatives). About 1 cm corresponds to about 3.6° of DF — [Physiopedia Weight-bearing Lunge Test](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Weight-bearing_Lunge_Test). Clinical templates use other cutoffs (<5 cm, <4 cm) — [Carepatron](https://www.carepatron.com/files/knee-to-wall-test.pdf); [Pabau](https://pabau.com/blog/knee-to-wall-test/).
- **GIRD:** Manske, Wilk et al. 2013 define anatomical GIRD as less than 18–20° IR loss with symmetric total rotational motion (TROM). Pathologic GIRD is IR loss >18–20° together with TROM loss >5° compared with the other side. TROM loss predicts shoulder injury in professional athletes — [PMC3811728](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3811728); [Wichita State SOAR](https://soar.wichita.edu/entities/publication/0f7187e9-52aa-4315-8bfd-72edc3759479).
- Rose & Noonan 2018 give the most common GIRD definition as >20° IR loss versus the contralateral side, with pathologic GIRD when TRM loss is >5°. Burkhart et al. 2003 used >25°. A 2019 review notes there is no consensus — [PMC5865552](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5865552); [PMC7534890](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7534890). Pathologic GIRD can also be classified purely on a >5° TROM asymmetry — [IJSPT volleyball study](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/141247-comparative-analysis-of-anatomical-and-pathological-glenohumeral-internal-rotation-deficit-among-volleyball-players-a-cross-sectional-study).
- **Modified Thomas test:** not a valid measure of hip extension unless pelvic tilt is controlled. With pelvic tilt controlled, it validly measures peak hip extension (n=29) — [PeerJ 2016, PMC4991856](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4991856/). Goniometric reliability was low (intra-rater ICC 0.51–0.54, inter-rater 0.30–0.65), while the trigonometric method was high (0.90–0.95) — [NATA J Athl Train 50(5):460](https://nata.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/attr/50/5/article-p460.pdf).
- **Active knee extension (AKE, 90-90 hamstring test):** intra-rater ICC 0.91, MDD about 7–8° (Neto et al. 2015, cited secondhand) — [Physiotutors 90-90 SLR](https://www.physiotutors.com/wiki/90-90-straight-leg-raise-test/). With a pressure biofeedback unit: inter-rater ICC 0.887–0.988, intra-rater 0.820–0.915 (n=16 males) — [KoreaScience](https://www.koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO201726868680751.pdf).

### Inferences
- Store the knee-to-wall result both in cm and as a tibial inclinometer angle. Use the angle for flags, because the distance depends on tibia length. A <10 cm or roughly <35–38° flag matches the clinical convention, but label it "convention, weak evidence".
- Compute GIRD from L/R IR and ER, giving IR deficit and TROM difference. The flag thresholds (IR deficit >20°, TROM difference >5°) should be configurable. (The symmetry logic itself belongs to another researcher's scope; only the ROM inputs matter here.)

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve reliability or norms for sit-and-reach (ACSM/YMCA norms), Apley scratch, passive SLR (normal about 70–90° is a textbook figure, not verified) or the original Thomas test norms (Harvey 1998). These need follow-up.
- I found no primary source for the "Hoch <38°" dorsiflexion cutoff.

## 5. Functional ROM for ADLs and stretching/mobility evidence (static vs dynamic, ACSM dose)

### Takeaway
Most ADLs need much less than full anatomical ROM: about 110–120° knee flexion, about 120–130° shoulder elevation and about 120–130° elbow flexion, with wrist motion of about 40° flexion/extension. These figures make practical rehab goals. ACSM recommends static stretching for each major muscle–tendon unit at least 2–3 days per week (daily is best): hold 10–30 s (30–60 s for older adults), 2–4 repetitions, about 60 s total per muscle group. Short static stretches (<60 s per muscle) cause only a small performance loss of about 1%.

### Cited Findings
- **Knee:** Rowe et al. 2000 (*Gait & Posture*; n=20, mean age 67, flexible electrogoniometry). Gait and slopes need <90° of knee flexion. Stairs and chairs need 90–120°. A bath needs about 135°. The authors suggest 110° as a reasonable rehab goal — [Strathclyde](https://pureportal.strath.ac.uk/en/publications/knee-joint-kinematics-in-gait-and-other-functional-activities-mea/); [Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/id/eprint/13519).
- TKA patient-education rules of thumb: 90° for stairs (100° to go down), 93° to rise from a chair, 105° to get out of a low chair — [eOrthopod](https://eorthopod.com/news/optimal-range-of-motion-after-total-knee-replacement/). (Secondary, low-tier source.)
- **Upper limb:** Gates et al. 2016 (*Am J Occup Ther* 70(1):7001350010p1; n=15, motion capture, 8 ADLs). Minimum ranges needed to complete all tasks — [AJOT](https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.5014/ajot.2016.015487); [PMC4690598](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4690598/):
  - Shoulder elevation 0–108°.
  - Shoulder horizontal abd/add −65° to 105°.
  - Humeral rotation −55° to 79°.
  - Elbow flexion 0–121°.
  - Forearm rotation −53° to 13°.
  - Wrist flex/ext −40° to 38°.
  - Wrist ulnar/radial deviation −28° to 38°.
  - Trunk flexion/extension 59°.
- A 2018 systematic review (36 studies) found that healthy people used up to full elbow flexion (150°) in personal care, eating and drinking. About 130° of shoulder flexion and abduction was needed — [Hanze summary](https://hbokennisbank.nl/details/hanzepure:oai:research.hanze.nl:publications/74628adc-6cbf-4733-a64c-9d7472e93e45) (as summarized in search results).
- **ACSM flexibility guidelines** (Table 5.6, *ACSM's Guidelines for Exercise Testing and Prescription*, 11th ed.):
  - Frequency of 2–3 days per week or more; daily is most effective.
  - Stretch to the point of tightness or slight discomfort.
  - Hold 10–30 s for most adults; 30–60 s may benefit older adults.
  - Cover each major muscle–tendon unit.
  - Source: [ACSM blog 2021](https://www.acsm.org/blog-detail/acsm-certified-blog/2021/03/18/stretching-and-flexibility-guidelines-update).
  - The 2–4 repetitions and 60 s total per muscle group come from the position-stand table, seen only through a secondary copy — [ACSM flexibility table (secondary)](https://www.anatomytrains.com/wp-content/uploads/manual/acsm.docx).
- **Static stretching and performance:** Behm, Blazevich, Kay & McHugh 2016, *Appl Physiol Nutr Metab* 41(1):1–11. Static stretching <60 s per muscle group gave a −1.1% performance change, versus −4.6% for ≥60 s. Adding dynamic activity after stretching removed any clear negative effect. Stretching within a warm-up followed by dynamic activity is recommended for ROM and injury reduction — [Northampton NECTAR](https://nectar.northampton.ac.uk/id/eprint/8154/); [ECU](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworkspost2013/2642/).

### Inferences
- The app can attach "functional target" markers to the ROM entry UI. Examples: knee flexion 90° (gait/stairs minimum), 110° (rehab goal) and 120° (chair/stairs comfortably); shoulder elevation about 110–130°; elbow flexion about 120–130°. Progress charts can then show both the normal-range band and the functional threshold.
- A dose tracker based on ACSM can be simple: muscle group × hold seconds × repetitions, summed to ≥60 s per group per session, ≥2–3 sessions per week.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve long-term (chronic) stretching meta-analyses comparing static, dynamic and PNF stretching for ROM gains (for example, Konrad 2023 and Thomas 2018 "dose-response"). Evidence on whether the ROM gain comes from increased stretch tolerance or tissue change was also not reviewed.
- I could not access the primary ACSM 11th/12th edition text. The ACSM 12th edition (2025) may have updated the table, which I did not check.
- Hip and ankle functional ROM for ADLs (for example, squatting or putting on socks) was not retrieved.
